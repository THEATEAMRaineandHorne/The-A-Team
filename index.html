Free appraisal

What is a free property appraisal, and how is it different from a registered valuation?

A property appraisal is a licensed agent's written estimate of the price your home is likely to sell for, based on recent comparable sales and current market conditions. A registered valuation is a paid, formal report from a registered valuer, and it is what lenders usually require.

Edita's appraisals are free and in writing, but they are not valuations.

FeatureAgent's appraisalRegistered valuation
Who prepares itA licensed real estate agentA registered valuer (Valuers Act 1948)

What it is based onRecent comparable sales and current buyer demandA formal valuation report on the property
Main useSetting price expectations, selling method and marketingLending, legal and insurance purposes
Accepted by lendersUsually notUsually yes
When you get itBefore you sign an agency agreementWhen you order one

Under REA rules, an agent must give you a written appraisal before you sign an agency agreement. It must realistically reflect current market conditions and be supported by comparable sales. If there are no comparable sales, the appraisal should say so.

Costs

How much does it cost to sell with Edita?

The main costs are commission plus GST, and any marketing you agree to. Edita's commission is usually lower than the tiered structure many agencies use, is only payable when your home sells, carries no administration fee and is confirmed in writing at the appraisal.

Many New Zealand agencies charge a tiered commission: a higher percentage on the first part of the sale price, a lower percentage on the balance, plus GST, and sometimes an administration fee. Before you sign, REA rules require the agent to give you a written estimate of the total commission, based on the likely sale price, and to say whether GST is included.

Marketing costs, such as photography, portal upgrades and Raine & Horne Amplify, are agreed separately and in writing before your campaign starts. Selling methods

Which selling method suits your home: auction, deadline sale or asking price?

It depends on how many buyers are likely to compete and whether they will need conditions. Auctions suit homes likely to draw several buyers ready to bid unconditionally, deadline sales suit homes where buyers may need finance or building-report conditions, and an asking price suits homes where a clear price brings more enquiry.

Auction

Buyers bid publicly on the day. A winning bid is unconditional, so buyers must arrange finance and reports beforehand.

Deadline sale

Buyers make written offers by a set date, without public bidding, and offers can include conditions.

Asking price or by negotiation

The price is shown or negotiated, and offers are often conditional.

Edita recommends a method in your written appraisal, based on buyer demand for similar homes nearby. REA rules require agents to explain the sale options, such as auction, tender or an advertised price, before you sign.

Preparing to sell

How should you prepare a West Auckland home for sale?

Start with paperwork and small fixes, not renovations. Order the LIM from Auckland Council early, check your title, fix minor maintenance, declutter, and get advice before spending on larger work.

LIM

Auckland Council has up to 10 working days to issue a LIM under the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987, so order it before you list.

Title

Cross-lease titles are common in older Auckland subdivisions. If a deck or extension is not shown on the flats plan, a buyer's lawyer may raise it, so have the title checked early.

Presentation

Light, space and street appeal matter more than new fittings. See home presentation advice and how to prepare your home for sale.

Spending

Ask which jobs buyers in your suburb actually pay for before you start any project.

Choosing an agent

How do you choose a real estate agent in West Auckland?

Interview at least three agents and compare evidence, not promises: recent comparable sales near you, how they reached their price, every cost in writing, who will run your campaign, and their plan if offers are slow. Then check each agent's licence on the REA public register.

Questions to ask every agent:

  • Which homes like mine have you sold nearby in the last six months, and for what?
  • How did you reach your price estimate?
  • What will I pay in commission, GST and marketing, in dollars?
  • Who will run the open homes and negotiate the offers?
  • What happens if there are no acceptable offers after the first few weeks?

Consumer NZ also recommends talking to at least three agents, asking each for an appraisal and comparing commission. For a fuller comparison, read the guide to real estate agents in West Auckland.

Problems

What if your home does not sell, or you want to change agents?

If offers are slow, the cause is usually price, presentation or marketing, and buyer feedback shows which one. If you want to change agents, check your agency agreement first: REA rules allow a residential sole agency longer than 90 days to be cancelled by either side at any time after 90 days.

During a campaign, Edita reports buyer feedback and enquiry quality so you can change course on evidence. Before you sign with a new agent, ask whether any buyer introduced by your previous agent could still trigger a commission. REA warns that signing a new sole agency while another is still in force carries a high risk of paying two commissions.

For the usual reasons a campaign stalls, read why some homes do not sell.

Timing

Should you sell now or wait?

Base the decision on your own timing and your next purchase, not on headline forecasts. If you are buying in the same market, a softer or stronger market usually affects your sale and your purchase in a similar way.

Many owners ask Edita for advice months before they list, so they know what to fix and when to go to market. For more, read should I sell my house now or wait? and the best time to sell in West Auckland.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

These questions are for homeowners who want to understand how Edita works, what makes a strong selling strategy and how to prepare before going to market.

Is an agent's appraisal the same as a valuation?

No. An appraisal is a licensed agent's written price estimate based on recent comparable sales, and Edita provides it free. A registered valuation is a paid report from a registered valuer, and it is what lenders usually ask for.

Do you pay anything if your home does not sell?

You pay no commission unless your home sells, and there is no administration fee. Any marketing you choose, such as Raine & Horne Amplify, is agreed and costed in writing before your campaign starts.

Can you cancel an agency agreement after signing?

Yes, within limits set by the Real Estate Authority. You can cancel a sole agency by 5pm on the first working day after you receive your copy, or within five working days if the agent approached you uninvited. A residential sole agency longer than 90 days can be cancelled by either side at any time after 90 days.

How can you check that Edita is licensed?

Search her name or licence number, 20027060, on the Real Estate Authority public register at publicregister.rea.govt.nz. The register shows each licensee's licence class, status and any disciplinary history from the last three years.

Is Edita a licensed agent or a licensed salesperson?

Edita holds an agent licence, number 20027060, which the Real Estate Authority public register lists as active. The agent class allows the holder to run a real estate business, and the public register is the official record of any licensee's class and status.

Will Edita work with buyers from other agencies?

Yes. Edita works with other agencies and their buyers when that helps the sale. A wider pool of buyers can mean more competition for your home.

Can you get advice if you are not ready to sell yet?

Yes. Many owners ask Edita for advice months ahead so they know what to fix, what to leave and when to list. There is no obligation to sign anything.

Do SuperGold Card holders get a discount?
