3/120 Rosebank Road

Preparing a rear unit and managing shared-property questions

The middle unit in a group of three had a garden, double glazing and a garage. Shared-area upkeep needed attention before buyers arrived.

We coordinated maintenance arrangements and advised on tidying, presentation and repairs raised through buyer feedback. A shared water-meter arrangement also needed to be explained during the campaign.

The outcome: Three offers were received at the deadline, and the property sold to a first-home buyer through a multi-offer process.

Sold 30 May 2022 · Price not disclosed

2/20 Blockhouse Bay Road

Improving the approach to a home down a shared driveway

This single-level two-bedroom home combined a leafy outlook, a fenced yard and a distinctive 1970s style. The long shared driveway needed cleaning and weeding, while surrounding presentation also influenced first impressions.

We suggested approaching neighbours together with the vendors, explaining the upcoming sale and encouraging everyone to help keep the shared approach tidy.

The local lesson: A home’s presentation starts before buyers reach its front door. This sale is recorded on 30 May 2022.

Sold 28 April 2017 · $552,750

7/29 Wingate Street

Making a renovated unit ready for every viewing

A renovated kitchen and bathroom, fresh finishes, a garage and storage were useful selling features. Road noise, limited privacy and nearby outdoor clutter were factors buyers also considered.

The vendor temporarily stayed with family, taking the dog along and clearing belongings from the home and garage. That made viewings easier to arrange and helped present the available space.

The outcome: The campaign attracted local and out-of-area buyers, and the property sold under the hammer.

Sold 17 April 2015 · $456,000

52 Saintly Lane

Coordinating a sale and the next purchase

This two-level home offered a gated setting, dedicated parking and a small backyard. Its proximity to the railway and two-level layout suited some buyers better than others.

The vendor also needed to find her next home. We helped with the search and offer process, then worked through the timing of both transactions.

The outcome: The sale and purchase settlement dates were aligned, helping the vendor make a coordinated move.

Sold 6 August 2017 · $702,000

53B Avondale Road

Continuing the campaign after a withdrawn offer

A rear position, flat outdoor area, three bedrooms and a study gave this relocated bungalow several points of appeal. Its unusual layout and visible signs of its relocation history needed to be considered in buyer conversations.

An early conditional offer was withdrawn before acceptance. We continued the campaign and followed up with a later buyer who returned for another viewing.

The outcome: The vendors accepted a subsequent offer with a three-day finance condition and moved within nine weeks. Our negotiation support considers conditions and timing alongside price.

Sold 19 April 2015 · $950,000

175 Avondale Road

Following up on interest after an auction passed in

The approximately 1,244 m² section, established garden and Whau River outlook distinguished this property from competing homes. The marketing focused on its setting, lifestyle and land.

After three weeks of viewings, the property went to auction and passed in without a bid. A buyer who had missed the auction called afterwards, and we arranged a viewing with her mother within an hour.

The outcome: An unconditional offer was received that day. The sale illustrates why follow-up remains important after the auction itself finishes.

Local advice grounded in Avondale homes

Our sales span rear units, a relocated bungalow, a two-level home near the railway and a property beside the Whau River. That variety is why a suburb-wide average cannot replace a property-specific discussion.

Rosebank Road: clarify shared arrangements

The 3/120 Rosebank Road campaign involved shared-area maintenance and a shared water meter. If your home has shared access or services, identify the relevant information early so buyers and their advisers can assess it.

Saintly Lane: understand the setting

At 52 Saintly Lane, railway proximity and a two-level layout influenced buyer suitability. A campaign should explain the home’s actual setting and layout so viewers can decide whether it meets their needs.

Avondale Road: show the point of difference

The Whau River garden at 175 Avondale Road and the rear bungalow at 53B Avondale Road needed different presentations. Photography and copy should explain what is distinctive about your particular home.

Questions Avondale sellers ask

Selling in Avondale: common questions

How do I choose the best real estate agent for my Avondale home?

Compare relevant local sales, seller reviews, marketing proposals and how each agent explains their pricing advice. Ask what they would do specifically for your property. Edita’s Avondale experience includes units, bungalows and waterfront property.

Is now a good time to sell a house in Avondale?

The answer depends on your circumstances, competing listings and recent comparable sales. Our Avondale market report provides local context, while an appraisal helps you consider how your particular home could compete.

How accurate are online property estimates for Avondale homes?

Online estimates are a starting point, but they may not fully reflect renovations, condition, access or presentation. A rear Rosebank Road unit and a riverside Avondale Road home need carefully selected comparisons rather than reliance on one automated figure.

Where can I get a free property appraisal in Avondale?

Edita provides free Avondale property appraisals. We consider recent comparable sales, your home’s features and current competition, then discuss a likely selling range and practical next steps. In-person appointments are arranged by agreement.

Should I use my council valuation as the asking price for my Avondale home?

Your council valuation should not be the only basis for setting an asking price. Recent comparable sales, condition and competing listings provide additional context. We explain the evidence behind our recommended range before you decide on a pricing strategy.

Why might a house in Avondale fail to sell?

Possible reasons include a price that buyers do not support, weak presentation, limited exposure or concerns about the property. We review enquiry, viewing feedback and competing homes to identify what may need attention.
