CV vs Market Value: What Your Auckland CV Means When You Sell

Your council CV is a rates figure based on 1 May 2024 values, not a price tag. Here is what it measures, why homes sell above and below it, and what to use instead when you decide on a price.

By Edita Andrijasevic | Raine & Horne • Licensed Agent (REAA 2008) • Guide for West Auckland sellers

Your CV is usually the first number you know about your home. It's printed on your rates notice and anyone can look it up online. But when it comes to CV vs market value, the two numbers answer different questions. Your Auckland Council CV is an estimate of what your home would have sold for on 1 May 2024, worked out so rates can be shared fairly. Market value is what a buyer will pay for it now.

This guide explains how the CV is set, why homes sell above and below it, what happened in two of Edita's own campaigns, and what to use instead when you're deciding on a price.

Quick answer

Your CV is a rates figure based on 1 May 2024 values and set by mass valuation, without anyone inspecting your home. It is a useful reference point, but it is not your sale price, and Auckland Council says it isn't meant for marketing or sales.

What is a CV in New Zealand?

A CV, or capital value, is the council's estimate of the most likely selling price of a property on a set date. Auckland's current CVs are based on values at 1 May 2024 and are used to share rates between properties, not to price homes for sale.

It has two parts. The land value (LV) is what the land alone would have sold for on that date, and the improvement value (IV) is the CV minus the land value. Auckland Council notes the IV doesn't necessarily reflect what your buildings are actually worth or what they would cost to replace. The CV also leaves out chattels, the movable items that usually go with a sale.

Is CV the same as RV or GV?

Yes. Auckland Council says capital value is also known as government valuation (GV) or rateable value (RV), so all three names refer to the same figure.

You'll still see GV and RV on older paperwork and in some property advertising, which is why the terms get mixed up.

How does Auckland Council work out your CV?

Your CV is set by mass valuation, which applies standard methods to many properties at once using recent sales and council records, rather than valuing each home on its own. Independent valuers QV and Opteon completed the 2024 values, and the Valuer-General checks them.

The council's own myth-busting page says the values were completed using information held by the council and valuers, not by individual inspection. The inputs are things like property type, location, land size, zoning, floor area and consented work such as renovations, new builds and subdivisions.

That means a mass valuation can't see much of what buyers react to: how well a renovation was done, the state of the kitchen and bathroom, the light, the outlook, the traffic noise, or how your home compares with what else is for sale that month. And if work was done without a building consent, the council has no record of it to use.

Is my CV the same as market value?

No. Your CV estimates what your home would have sold for on 1 May 2024, while market value is what a buyer will pay for it today, as it is now. Auckland Council says plainly that the 2024 values are not current market value and are not for marketing or sales.

The two drift apart for three reasons:

  • Time. The values are based on 1 May 2024 but were only released in June 2025, and every sale since then has happened in a different market.
  • Method. A mass valuation works from records. A buyer walks through your front door.
  • Competition. Buyers weigh your home against the other homes for sale that weekend, and against what similar homes have recently sold for.

Auckland Council's own economists have made the same point. When the 2021 values came out, the council's senior economist said they are done on mass appraisal and are not market value, and that anyone after a market value should contact a local valuer.

Why do homes sell above or below their CV?

Because a sale reflects today's buyers, today's competing listings and everything a mass valuation can't see, while the CV is a records-based estimate of one past date. When the new values came out in June 2025, interest.co.nz found that only 35% of Auckland homes sold at auction reached or beat their new RV, even though residential values had dropped 9% on average.

The share moves around. In one week of July 2026, interest.co.nz's count of auction sales from around the country had 48% selling at or above their rating valuation. Auctions are only a slice of all sales, but the point holds: whether a home lands above or below its CV says as much about the CV as it does about the home.

For an individual home, the gap mostly comes down to what the CV can't see: condition, presentation, layout, outlook, the price bracket buyers are shopping in, and how many similar homes are competing for the same buyers.

Can a home sell above its CV in a slow market?

Yes. In two of Edita's own deadline sale campaigns, a Titirangi home sold for $70,000 above its capital value in 2024 and a Blockhouse Bay home drew three offers above its CV in 2026, but in both cases buyer demand and the terms of the offer decided the result, not the CV.

Two campaigns, one lesson

In Titirangi, the owners of 773 South Titirangi Road wanted a result above their capital value at a time when the offers they had received were below their expectations. Edita launched it as a deadline sale on 6 August 2024. Three offers were presented and not accepted, and Edita kept following up buyers until the right one came through. It sold on 24 September 2024 for $1,320,000, which was $70,000 above its capital value. That CV came from the 2021 revaluation, because the 2024 values weren't used for rates until July 2025.

In Blockhouse Bay, 60 Kay Drive launched as a deadline sale on 28 May 2026, in a slower, buyer-sensitive market. It drew eight offers, three of them above the capital value. The owners accepted a strong unconditional offer rather than the highest one, because certainty mattered most for their next move, and it sold for $1,130,000 after 20 days.

In both sales, the CV didn't decide the result. Buyer demand, the campaign and the terms of the offer did.

You can read the full stories on Edita's Titirangi page and Blockhouse Bay page, and see how a deadline sale works. Each was one completed campaign and doesn't guarantee the same outcome for another home.

Should I use my CV to set my asking price?

No. Treat it as one reference point, not the price: the Real Estate Authority tells agents to avoid an unrealistic focus on rateable value and not to use it in advertising where it doesn't realistically reflect market value.

Pricing to the CV can go wrong both ways. If the CV sits above what buyers will pay, a price anchored to it can leave your home sitting on the market while buyers wait for it to drop. If the CV sits below, you may set your expectations lower than your home deserves.

A proper appraisal starts from the evidence instead. Under the REA's rules, an agent's appraisal must be in writing, realistically reflect current market conditions and be supported by comparable sales of similar properties in similar locations. Do buyers and banks look at my CV?

Buyers can look up any Auckland property's CV online by address, so it's safest to assume they know yours. Banks are a different matter: Auckland Council says its valuations should not be used for insurance or mortgage purposes.

A CV well above your likely sale price can make negotiations harder, even though it says nothing about today's market. Your agent should be ready to explain the gap to buyers with recent sales evidence.

Will my CV change if I renovate or subdivide?

It can. Auckland Council can make a supplementary valuation when a new title is issued, land is amalgamated, or buildings are added, altered or demolished, but the new figure is still assessed against 1 May 2024 values, not today's market.

So a renovation might lift your CV between revaluations, but the CV still won't tell you what that renovation is worth to buyers now.

Can I still object to my Auckland CV?

Not for the 2024 values. Objections closed on 25 July 2025, and Auckland Council received 10,500 objections, around 1.7% of its 630,000 ratepayers.

An objection can raise or lower a value, and the council says a revision isn't guaranteed. A higher CV doesn't automatically mean higher rates either: what matters is how your value moved compared with the average change.

Councils must revalue every three years. Auckland Council's revaluation page doesn't yet give dates for the next set of values, so check there before relying on a timeline.

How do I find out what my home is worth now?

Ask for a written appraisal built on recent comparable sales and today's competing listings, or engage a registered valuer if you need a formal valuation. Edita's appraisals are free, in writing, and come with the sales evidence behind the range.
