Real Estate Salesperson vs Agent: Who Is Responsible for Your Sale?

Anyone who sells houses gets called an agent, but in New Zealand there are three different real estate licences. Here is what each one means, who is responsible for your sale, and how to check anyone's licence yourself.

By Edita Andrijasevic | Raine & Horne • Licensed Agent (REAA 2008) • Guide for West Auckland sellers

In New Zealand, people call anyone who sells houses a "real estate agent". Legally, that isn't quite right. The Real Estate Authority (REA) licenses three different classes of people, and the difference between a real estate salesperson vs agent comes down to something every seller should care about: who is accountable for the work.

This guide explains the three licences, who supervises whom, and how to check anyone's licence yourself in about a minute.

Is a real estate agent the same as a salesperson in NZ?

No. A salesperson holds the entry-level licence and can only do real estate work for or on behalf of a licensed agent, while an agent holds the highest licence and can run a real estate business in their own right. In everyday speech both get called "agents", but on the REA public register they are different licence classes.

There is also a middle class, branch manager, which sits between the two.

The question sellers ask Edita most

At appraisals, one question comes up again and again: "If something goes wrong, who is actually responsible for my sale?" Edita's answer is short. She holds an Agent licence and runs her own Raine & Horne office, so the person responsible for your sale is the person sitting at your kitchen table.

What are the three real estate licence classes in NZ?

The three classes are salesperson, branch manager and agent. Each step up needs a higher New Zealand Certificate in Real Estate, and branch managers and agents also need at least three years of licensed experience.

Licence What it allows Qualification Experience Supervision
Salesperson Real estate work for or on behalf of a licensed agent NZ Certificate in Real Estate (Salesperson) (Level 4) No minimum stated Must be supervised by an agent or branch manager
Branch manager Salesperson work, plus supervising salespeople NZ Certificate in Real Estate (Level 5) At least 3 years licensed Can supervise salespeople
Agent Everything above, plus running an agency alone, in a partnership or as a company's eligible officer NZ Certificate in Real Estate (Level 6) At least 3 years licensed Can supervise and employ salespeople

Source: REA's pages on what each licence class can do and the qualifications each one needs.

Who supervises a real estate salesperson?

Every salesperson must be supervised by an agent or a branch manager whenever they do real estate work, however long they have been in the industry. REA says the ultimate responsibility for that supervision sits with the designated supervisor.

That supervisor has to make sure the salesperson is following the law, whether the salesperson is an employee or an independent contractor. So when a salesperson lists your home, there is always a second licensed person behind the work.

Who is responsible if something goes wrong with my sale?

Any licensed person handling your sale can be held to account through the Real Estate Authority, and if they are a salesperson, their supervisor is also responsible for how they were supervised. If they hold an Agent licence, there is no one above them in the licence chain, so the accountability sits with them.

For most sales, nothing goes wrong. But you are handing over the biggest thing you own, so it is reasonable to want one clear name.

Does the licence class matter when choosing who sells my home?

It tells you about accountability and supervision, not about skill. Plenty of salespeople are experienced and very good at what they do, so the licence class should be one question among several, not the deciding one.

Local sales, how they price and market, how often they will update you, and what past sellers say will tell you more about the result you are likely to get. The licence tells you who answers for the work.

How do I check if a real estate agent is licensed?

Search the REA public register by the person's name or licence number. It shows their licence class, whether the licence is active, and any complaints upheld against them in the last three years.

  1. Go to the REA public register.
  2. Search by individual name, business name or licence number. You can narrow a name search by location.
  3. Open the result and check the licence class (salesperson, branch manager or agent) and that the status is active.
  4. Look for any complaints upheld in the last three years.

How can I tell if an agent is legitimate?

Check that both the person and the agency appear on the REA register with active licences before you sign anything. It is illegal to do real estate agency work without a licence, and if you deal with an unlicensed person, REA will be unable to help you if things go wrong.

The register lists licensed companies as well as individuals, so you can check the agency and the person in the same place.

What should I ask an agent before signing?

Ask about their local sales, how they market, what it will cost, and who you will actually be dealing with. The government's Settled.govt.nz guide suggests asking for references from sellers whose properties they have sold in the last three months.

Questions worth adding to your list:

  • What licence do you hold?
  • If you are a salesperson, who supervises you?
  • Who will actually handle my open homes and buyer calls?

Where can I complain about a real estate agent in NZ?

Complaints about any licensed real estate professional go to the Real Estate Authority. You can contact REA on 0800 367 732, by email at info@rea.govt.nz, or through its online complaint page.

Knowing this exists matters even if you never use it. It is part of what makes a licence worth checking.
