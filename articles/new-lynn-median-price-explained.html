Why the New Lynn Median Price Doesn't Match Standalone Home Values

Your neighbour's house sold for well above the New Lynn "median" you saw online. Here's why that headline number and your home's actual value aren't the same thing.

By Edita Andrijasevic · Published 31 August 2026 · Updated 31 August 2026

A New Lynn homeowner emailed Edita last month after checking a property website. "The median for my suburb is $820,000, but my neighbour's house, smaller than mine, just sold for $980,000. What's going on?" It's a good question. Nothing is wrong with the data. The median simply isn't measuring what most people assume it measures.

Here's the short version, then the full explanation. For the latest REINZ-sourced numbers, see the New Lynn property market report, updated every month.

The quick answer

The New Lynn median is one number covering standalone homes, townhouses, units and apartments together. New Lynn has a lot of the latter three, and they sell for less. Blend them all in, and the median drops below what a standalone house on its own land usually fetches. Compare your standalone home to other standalone homes, not to the suburb-wide blend.

Why the number looks low

New Lynn is a town centre suburb. Train and bus links, LynnMall, and a wave of medium-density development mean the sales mix here looks different to, say, Green Bay or Titirangi. The monthly market report breaks this down: residences, townhouses, units, and a smaller apartment segment, each selling in its own price band.

Line those bands up and the pattern is obvious. Apartments sell for a fraction of what a house does. Units and townhouses sit somewhere in between. Fold them all into one suburb-wide figure and the result gets pulled down. Not because houses lost value, but because more of the sales sample is made up of cheaper property types.

Tenure matters just as much

Freehold sells for more than cross-lease or strata, on average, and that's true inside New Lynn regardless of what the house itself looks like. So when you're sizing up your own home against the market, you need two filters, not one: the right property type, and the right tenure. Miss either and the comparison stops meaning much.

So what does this mean if you own a standalone home?

Use the median to read the direction of the market: up, down, flat. Not the value of your house. In the latest 12-month period, residences recorded a noticeably higher median than townhouses, units or apartments in New Lynn. That's the gap your neighbour's email was really asking about.

Getting a real number means a property-specific appraisal: your home matched against recent sales of a similar property type, tenure, bedroom count and condition, in a similar spot. That's a different exercise to reading a headline figure off a news article.

Where the current number lives

We haven't quoted a specific median in this guide on purpose. REINZ data moves month to month, and a hardcoded figure here would be stale within weeks. The New Lynn property market report is where that number actually gets updated, along with the full property-type breakdown.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the New Lynn median lower than what my standalone home is worth?

Because it isn't measuring your house alone — it's an average of every sale type in the suburb, including townhouses, units and apartments that sell for less. Compare your home against other standalone houses, not the blended figure.

Where can I find the current New Lynn median price?

On the New Lynn property market report. It's rebuilt from REINZ data every month, so it's the number to check, not this guide.

Does tenure (freehold, cross-lease, strata) change things too?

Yes, and by a lot. Freehold consistently sells above cross-lease and strata in New Lynn. Factor tenure in alongside property type when you're sizing up your own home.

I own a standalone home in New Lynn — how should I actually use the median?

As a weather vane, not a valuation. It tells you whether the market's warming up or cooling down. What your house is worth needs a proper comparison against similar recent sales — a job for an appraisal, not a headline number.

Do auction sales and private-treaty sales get lumped together in that figure?

They're reported separately in the full market report, and for good reason — auction campaigns tend to attract a different mix of properties than private treaty. Comparing the two head-to-head doesn't tell you much about which method gets a better price.
