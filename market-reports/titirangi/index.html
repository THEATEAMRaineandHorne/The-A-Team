Market Update

What Is Happening In Titirangi?

Prices are fairly steady for the homes most owners are interested in. Homes are also selling a little faster, but there have been fewer sales.

Over the past 12 months, Titirangi’s median sale price was $1,046,942. A year earlier it was $1,020,000. The average sale price was $1,129,428.

For standard residences, the median was $1,049,442. A year earlier it was $1,070,500. For many homeowners, that is the more useful comparison.

Homes took a median of 45 days to sell, down from 50 days. There were 206 sales, compared with 285 the year before.

Local Market

Titirangi Market Report

Titirangi is never a one-price suburb. Two homes only a few streets apart can sell very differently.

Sun, bush, privacy, views, access and parking all matter. So do maintenance and the condition of decks, retaining and drainage.

Buyers are still looking. They are more cautious with homes that need work or feel over-priced. A good home, priced properly, can still attract strong interest.

Property Type And Tenure

Most sales were standard residences. There were 186, with a median of $1,049,442. A year earlier the residence median was $1,070,500.

Only 6 townhouses and 4 units sold. Those numbers are too small to read much into their medians.

Freehold homes made up most of the market, with 175 sales. There were 14 cross-lease sales.

Bedroom Sales

Three-bedroom homes had a median of $959,500. Four-bedroom homes were higher at $1,130,000.

Five-bedroom homes had a median of $1,366,250, although only 14 sold. The two-bedroom sample was also small.

In Titirangi, bedroom count only tells part of the story. Sun, access, views, parking and condition can make a big difference to price.

What Buyers Are Looking At

Titirangi buyers love privacy, bush and character. But they also look closely at the practical side of the property.

Driveways, parking, dampness, drainage, decks and retaining can all affect a buyer’s confidence. Homes that feel warm, dry and well cared for are easier to sell.

Sale Method

There were 50 auction sales. Their median price was $1,147,500 and the median selling time was 37 days.

Another 156 properties sold by private treaty or negotiation. Their median was $1,004,000, with a median selling time of 48 days.

That does not make auction the right choice for every home. The method should suit the property and the likely buyers.

Advice For Titirangi Sellers

  • Use recent comparable sales. Do not rely on the suburb median alone.
  • Have the LIM, title and consent information ready early.
  • Deal with obvious maintenance before photos and open homes where possible.
  • Show the things Titirangi buyers value: sun, privacy, access, parking and outdoor living.
  • Listen to buyer feedback and adjust quickly if the market is telling you something.

Local Factors Affecting Titirangi

  • Waitākere Ranges lifestyle, native bush and privacy
  • Titirangi Village and strong local identity
  • Sun, outlook, access, parking and land usability
  • Drainage, retaining, decks and weather exposure
  • Insurance, consent records and renovation cost
  • Comparison with Green Bay, New Lynn, Glen Eden and nearby lifestyle areas

What We Expect Over The Next Few Months

I expect Titirangi to stay fairly steady rather than suddenly take off. Buyers are there, but they are choosy.

Good family homes should still attract interest. Price will be important, especially for homes with difficult access, maintenance issues or incomplete paperwork.

The best guide for a seller will be the homes competing with yours when you come to market.

Questions Homeowners Are Asking Right Now

These are the questions that matter when you are deciding whether to sell, stay, renovate or hold.

Your Home, Equity And Selling Price

How much extra can a flat driveway, north-facing sun or a good outlook add to my Titirangi sale price?

There is no fixed percentage. In Titirangi these things can make a real difference because access, sun and outlook vary so much from one street to the next. The best guide is recent sales with similar land, access and position.

Has Titirangi held its value better than nearby suburbs because there is less intensive development?

It is hard to put one number on that. Titirangi’s character and lower-density feel are part of its appeal, but they are not the only reason prices move. Over the past year the suburb median rose 2.6%, while the median for standard residences was 2.0% lower.

Are Titirangi buyers paying above or below CV?

CV is a reference point, not a price target. Among the sales in this report that had a valuation figure recorded, most sold below that figure. Around 59% sold below it, 38% above it and 3% at it. The individual property still matters far more than the CV.

Timing And Sale Strategy

If 45 days is the median, why do some Titirangi homes sell in three weeks and others take much longer?

Usually it comes back to price, presentation and the property itself. Difficult access, maintenance, dampness, retaining or incomplete paperwork can slow buyers down. A well-presented home at the right price can move much faster.

Only 206 properties sold over the past year. Does that give me an advantage if I list before spring gets busy?

Not automatically. Fewer sales means lower turnover, but it does not tell us how many homes were listed. The real advantage comes when there are few properties competing with yours at the same price point.

Auction or negotiation — which is working better in Titirangi?

There were 50 auction sales in this report, with a median of $1,147,500 and a 37-day median selling time. Private treaty and negotiation recorded 156 sales, with a $1,004,000 median and 48-day selling time. They are different groups of homes, so those figures do not prove auction is better. The right method depends on your property and how much buyer competition we expect.

Bush, Hillsides And Property Condition

How much are landslip, hillside and insurance concerns affecting Titirangi buyers?

Buyers are asking more questions about natural hazards, retaining and insurance. That matters most when a property is steep, has known issues or needs further investigation. It does not mean every bush property is discounted. Good information helps buyers feel more comfortable.

Should I fix minor maintenance before selling, or put the home on the market as-is?

Small visible problems are usually worth dealing with. Buyers can see a leaking gutter, tired deck or obvious moisture issue and start wondering what else might be wrong. Bigger work needs to be judged case by case before spending money.

How do Significant Ecological Area (SEA) rules affect my Titirangi property?

SEA rules can affect vegetation removal and some development work. They do not create the same restriction on every property. For some buyers, protected bush is part of the appeal. For others, future plans matter more. The overlay and rules should be checked for the individual site before making any promises about development.

What To Do Next

If I sell now, would my equity go further if I downsized to an apartment or moved further west?

It can, but the answer depends on what you buy next. An apartment may free up more cash, but body corporate costs and the building itself matter. Moving further west may buy more land or a larger home, but travel time and upkeep can be higher. Work from your likely net sale proceeds, not the suburb median.

Could I keep my Titirangi home and rent it out instead of selling?

Possibly. Some larger family homes are asking around $850 a week or more, but $1,200 is not a safe suburb-wide assumption. Rent depends on the home, condition, parking, bedrooms and location. Get a current rental appraisal before deciding to hold.
